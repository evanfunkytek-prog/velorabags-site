Custom Handbag MOQ, Sampling and Lead Time: What to Plan For

Nov 25, 2026 • Handbag Planning Guide

A handbag programme usually starts with three commercial questions: how many pieces must I order, what does the sample cost, and when will bulk production be ready? The answers depend on whether you are buying a standard style or developing a custom construction.

Quick reference: standard styles from 100 pcs; custom development from 300 pcs; standard-style samples free; custom samples USD 60–150; sampling 3–5 days after artwork approval; bulk production 15–25 days after sample approval. The sample fee is refunded when the order is placed or when cumulative quantity reaches 1,000 pcs.

What the MOQ actually covers

MOQ is the minimum quantity a factory can produce while keeping the material, cutting, hardware, sampling and inspection process commercially viable. It is not only a sales threshold. A small run can require the same fabric minimum, the same hardware order, the same line setup and the same quality checks as a larger one.

For standard handbag styles, the starting point is 100 pcs. For a custom handbag developed around your own shape, dimensions, material mix or hardware, the starting point is 300 pcs. If you need several colours or material combinations, ask the quotation to state exactly how the quantity is calculated. A clear MOQ basis avoids a later disagreement about what counts toward the minimum.

How sampling fees and refunds work

Standard-style samples are free. A custom sample is quoted at USD 60–150 depending on the construction, materials and branding. The fee covers the work of making a sample that is specific to your design rather than pulling a stock item from the shelf.

The custom sample fee is refunded when the bulk order is placed or when cumulative quantity reaches 1,000 pcs. Ask for that condition to be written on the quotation. Also confirm whether courier transit is included, because a sample sent by DHL or FedEx is a separate cost from the sample itself.

Sampling takes 3–5 days after artwork approval. The trigger matters: the clock starts when the artwork, dimensions, material direction and sample fee are confirmed, not when the first WhatsApp message is sent. If you need a second round, allow time for comments, revision and a new courier dispatch.

When the production clock starts

Bulk production takes 15–25 days after sample approval. In practice, the start trigger should be the later of sample approval, deposit receipt, final artwork and availability of any customer-supplied material. A purchase order alone does not start production if the approved sample or deposit is still outstanding.

Shipping transit is separate from production. Sea, rail, air and courier options have different transit times, cut-offs and clearance requirements. If you are working to a retail launch, plan backwards from the date the goods must be in your warehouse, not from the date they leave the factory.

A launch calendar should include a buffer for sample revisions and courier delays. If the date cannot move, tell the factory at the brief stage. A realistic plan is more useful than an optimistic one that assumes every approval happens on the first attempt.

What changes the quote

Two handbags with the same outline can have very different costs. The main drivers are the outer material, lining, interlining, hardware, closure, number of panels, stitching complexity, logo method, packing and order quantity. Inspection and testing requirements can also change the price if they require external laboratory work or additional production checks.

  • Material: full PU, PU-trimmed textile, canvas and linen have different costs and cutting behaviour.
  • Hardware: the number of metal parts, finish and brand marking affect both cost and lead time.
  • Construction: more panels, pockets, zips and edge finishes mean more operations.
  • Branding: embossing, foil, metal plates, embroidery and printing use different tooling and setup.
  • Packing: dust bags, inserts, hangtags and retail cartons add cost and production steps.
  • Quantity: the confirmed order quantity determines material buying and line efficiency.

Questions to ask before paying a deposit

  1. Is the MOQ quoted for a standard style or a custom construction?
  2. Does the sample fee include the sample, or is courier transit separate?
  3. What exactly triggers the sample refund?
  4. When does the 15–25 day production window start?
  5. Which Incoterm is quoted, and what is excluded?
  6. What inspection and packing steps are included?
  7. Which tests or certificates are available for this material and market?
  8. What happens if artwork, hardware or quantities change after approval?

Plan the commercial side before the creative side gets expensive

MOQ, sample cost and lead time are not obstacles to good design. They are the constraints that make a programme repeatable. Once those numbers are clear, the sampling team can focus on the details that matter: proportion, material, hardware, branding and the standard the bulk order must meet.
