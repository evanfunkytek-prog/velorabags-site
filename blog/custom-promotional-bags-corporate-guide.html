Custom Promotional Bags: Choosing Non-Woven, Woven PP, Paper or Fabric

Nov 25, 2026 • Promotional Bag Sourcing Guide

A promotional bag is part advertising, part product and part brand experience. The substrate you choose determines how it feels at the event, how long it stays in use and what the recipient infers about the brand. The cheapest bag is rarely the best value if it tears before the campaign ends.

This guide compares the main custom promotional bag routes and the specification details that should be agreed before sampling. It is written for marketing teams, distributors and retail buyers who need a repeatable brief rather than a catalogue order.

1. Start with distribution and reuse

Ask where the bag will be handed out, what it will carry and how likely it is to be reused. A lightweight bag for a one-day conference has a different brief from a retail shopper designed to be used weekly. The distribution plan also affects size, handle length, print area and packing.

2. Compare the four common substrates

Do not compare substrates on unit price alone. A non-woven bag may be the lowest-cost route for a large one-off event; a canvas or jute bag may deliver more impressions per unit because it is kept and reused. Paper can carry a premium retail signal that a synthetic bag cannot. Choose the substrate that supports the campaign objective.

3. Match the print method to the material

Screen printing is direct, durable and economical for bold graphics with a limited number of colours. Heat transfer handles more detailed artwork and can be cost-effective at smaller quantities. Laminated full-colour printing gives photographic detail on non-woven and woven PP, but the lamination becomes part of the bag’s surface and should be considered in the hand feel and recycling story.

Paper bags support offset printing, foil stamping and embossing for a more premium finish. Canvas and jute can take screen print, embroidery, woven labels and patches. Ask for a proof on the actual substrate, not only on white paper. Dark backgrounds, coated surfaces and textured fabrics can change colour and line weight.

4. Write a production-ready promotional bag spec

  • Size: width, height, depth and handle drop in centimetres.
  • Material: substrate, weight or gsm, colour and finish.
  • Handle: material, attachment and reinforcement.
  • Print: method, colours, Pantone references and placement.
  • Packing: polybag, header card, barcode, carton marks and pallet requirements.
  • Compliance: material declarations, test reports and destination-market requirements.

If the bag is part of a retail programme, include the barcode and packing sequence in the brief. If it is a corporate gift, confirm whether individual polybags, tissue paper or a printed belly band are required. These details change the packing time and the carton count.

5. Sampling, QC and lead time

Custom samples are USD 60–150 depending on the construction; standard-style samples are free. The sample fee is refunded when the order is placed or when cumulative quantity reaches 1,000 pcs. Sampling takes 3–5 days after artwork approval, and bulk production takes 15–25 days after sample approval.

Promotional bag inspection should match the failure mode. Non-woven and woven PP need seam peel and handle pull checks; printed bags need print adhesion and rub tests; paper bags need handle pull and glue bond checks. Confirm the sampling plan before production, especially for large campaigns where a reprint is expensive.

6. Control the cost drivers

The main cost drivers are substrate weight, bag size, handle type, number of print colours, lamination, packing and order quantity. A larger bag uses more material; a laminated full-colour print adds a production step; a reinforced handle adds labour. If the budget is fixed, ask the factory to quote two or three constructions rather than forcing one material to meet an unrealistic target.

Promotional bag RFQ checklist

  1. Campaign date, distribution channel and expected quantity
  2. Substrate preference or performance requirement
  3. Finished size, gusset and handle drop
  4. Logo artwork, colours and print placement
  5. Packing, labels, barcode and carton marks
  6. Destination market and required documentation
  7. Target unit price and delivery date
