Custom Travel Bags and Backpacks: How to Specify an OEM Program

Nov 25, 2026 • Travel & Backpack Sourcing Guide

Travel bags and backpacks are multi-part products. The shell, lining, foam, zips, hardware, straps and reinforcement all have to work together, and the weakest component often decides whether the product survives a real trip. A good OEM brief therefore starts with load, use case and failure points, not only with a reference image.

This guide covers custom duffels, weekender bags, travel shoulder bags and laptop backpacks. It is intended for brands, retailers and corporate buyers who need a repeatable specification before sampling.

1. Choose the format around the trip

A duffel or weekender is built for volume and access; a travel shoulder bag prioritises carrying comfort and quick-reach compartments; a laptop backpack needs a structured sleeve, a padded back panel and balanced straps. A trolley case is a different construction again, with a hard or soft shell and a telescopic handle system.

Trolley caseCabin or checked luggageShell material, wheels, telescopic handle, lock and lining

2. Specify the shell and lining

Common travel bag shells include 600D polyester, recycled RPET, cotton canvas, 1680D ballistic nylon and PU leather. The shell choice affects abrasion resistance, weight, hand feel and cost. A PU coating can add water resistance, but the coating, backing and seam treatment should be confirmed on the sample.

The lining is not a cosmetic detail. It affects the internal structure, pocket installation, wipe-clean performance and weight. Specify the lining colour, weight and any special requirement such as a bright interior for easy packing or a food-safe lining for a cooler compartment.

3. Design for load and movement

A travel bag is lifted, dragged, overpacked and thrown into a hold. The base, handle anchors, strap attachments and zip ends need reinforcement. For a backpack, the shoulder straps and back panel carry most of the load; for a duffel, the handle and base take the stress.

  • Reinforced base: extra fabric, board or bartack at the highest-wear points.
  • Strap anchors: box-and-X stitching, bartacks or reinforced patches.
  • Zips: size, slider, puller and end reinforcement matched to the load.
  • Laptop protection: foam padding, a suspended sleeve and a soft-touch lining.
  • Travel features: trolley sleeve, shoe compartment, wet pocket, key leash or bottle pocket.

4. Choose branding and hardware together

Embroidery, rubber patches, metal badges, screen printing and woven labels all suit different surfaces and price positions. Hardware finish should be selected at the same time as the shell and zip, because a mismatch between the logo plate, rings, buckles and zips is immediately visible.

Confirm the logo placement on the actual sample, not only on a flat artwork. A backpack logo may need to sit between seams or avoid a curved panel; a duffel logo may need to remain readable when the bag is full. Ask for a photo of the finished placement before bulk production.

5. Test the product like a traveller

Useful travel bag inspection points include zip cycles, strap pull, load tests, seam strength, drop tests and abrasion. The exact test plan should match the product: a duffel may be loaded to 20 kg, while a backpack strap may be pulled to 25 kg. A laptop sleeve should be checked with the intended device size, not only measured flat.

Before shipment, inspect the first output against the approved sample, then check the production run for stitching, hardware, lining, logo placement and packing. 6. MOQ, sampling and lead time

Standard travel bag and backpack styles start from 100 pcs; custom development starts from 300 pcs. Standard-style samples are free; custom samples are USD 60–150 depending on the construction. The sample fee is refunded when the order is placed or when cumulative quantity reaches 1,000 pcs.

Sampling takes 3–5 days after artwork approval. Bulk production takes 15–25 days after sample approval. The production clock should start only when the approved sample, deposit, final artwork and any customer-supplied components are in place. Shipping transit is separate.

Travel bag RFQ checklist

  1. Format, capacity and finished dimensions
  2. Shell material, coating and lining
  3. Padded laptop size or internal compartment layout
  4. Zip, hardware, strap and handle specification
  5. Logo method and placement
  6. Load, drop, abrasion or other test requirements
  7. Target quantity, sample date and delivery date
  8. Packing, labels, barcodes and export carton marks
