Custom Canvas Tote Bags: A Specification Guide for Buyers

Nov 25, 2026 • Canvas Tote Sourcing Guide

A canvas tote looks simple, but the specification behind a good one is not. Fabric weight, shrinkage, handle attachment, gusset depth, lining, print method and packing all affect how the bag feels, how long it lasts and what it costs. Two totes that look identical in a reference photo can quote at very different prices.

This guide is written for buyers developing a custom canvas programme for retail, gifting, hospitality or brand merchandise. It focuses on the details that should be settled before sampling so the factory can quote a construction that will still work in bulk.

1. Start with fabric weight and finish

Cotton canvas for bags commonly ranges from 8 oz to 16 oz. Heavier fabric feels more substantial and holds a stiffer shape; lighter fabric gives a softer, more foldable tote. The right choice depends on the load, the retail price and the hand feel the brand wants.

Ask for a swatch and confirm whether the canvas is pre-shrunk. Shrinkage matters if the bag will be washed by the end user or if the finished dimensions must remain stable. Also confirm the finish: natural greige, washed, garment-dyed, PU-coated or another treatment. Organic or recycled cotton options may be available, but the claim should be supported by the material documentation, not just the word “eco” on the artwork.

2. Choose the handle and attachment

The handle is one of the first parts to fail on a loaded tote. Specify the material, width, length and attachment method. Self-fabric handles look integrated; cotton webbing is strong and practical; padded cotton is more comfortable; leather-look trim can lift the perceived value.

Ask how the handle is secured. An X-box stitch, bartack or reinforced patch distributes the load better than a simple straight seam. Confirm the handle drop in centimetres and test it with the intended load. For a retail tote, a handle pull test and a visual check of the seam are basic acceptance criteria.

3. Specify the shape, gusset and interior

Give the factory width, height and depth in centimetres, not just a picture. A flat tote and a gusseted tote use different cutting and sewing operations. If the bag needs a base reinforcement, an internal pocket, a zip closure or a lining, put those details in the tech pack.

  • Dimensions: width x height x depth, with a tolerance.
  • Gusset: flat, side gusset, bottom gusset or boxed base.
  • Closure: open top, magnetic snap, zip or drawstring.
  • Interior: lining, open pocket, zip pocket, card slot or key leash.
  • Reinforcement: base board, interlining, bartack or rivet.

4. Plan the print before the sample

Canvas accepts screen printing, heat transfer, embroidery and woven labels. Screen printing is economical for bold, limited-colour graphics; heat transfer can handle more detailed artwork; embroidery adds texture and perceived quality. The method should be chosen together with the artwork, because line weight, colour count and placement all affect the result.

Send vector artwork and a Pantone reference where colour matters. Confirm the print area, the number of colours and whether the artwork will be printed on one or both sides. Ask for a print-adhesion or rub test on the actual fabric, especially for dark colours and coated canvas. A print that looks correct on a white swatch may not perform the same way on a dyed or washed ground.

5. MOQ, sampling and production

Standard canvas styles start from 100 pcs; custom development starts from 300 pcs. Standard-style samples are free, while custom samples are USD 60–150 depending on the construction. The sample fee is refunded when the order is placed or when cumulative quantity reaches 1,000 pcs.

Sampling takes 3–5 days after artwork approval. Bulk production takes 15–25 days after sample approval. Those dates assume the artwork, deposit and any customer-supplied labels or packaging are ready. Courier transit is separate and can be arranged by DHL, FedEx or your nominated carrier.

6. Inspect the details that fail in use

Canvas totes are usually inspected for handle pull, seam strength, stitch density, print adhesion, colour fastness and dimensional accuracy. Check the inside as carefully as the outside: loose lining, incomplete seam coverage and sharp hardware edges are common causes of returns. Packing should protect the print and keep the handles from creasing.

Put this in your canvas tote RFQ

  1. Finished width, height, depth and handle drop in centimetres
  2. Canvas weight, finish and fibre claim
  3. Handle material and attachment method
  4. Lining, pockets, closure and reinforcement
  5. Logo method, artwork, colours and print placement
  6. Target quantity, target price and delivery date
  7. Packing method, labels and barcode requirements

If you are comparing canvas with jute or non-woven, send the use case and budget rather than a single material name. We can recommend a construction that fits the way the bag will actually be distributed.
