How to Develop a Custom Handbag from Sketch to Sample

Nov 25, 2026 • Handbag Development Guide

A handbag sample is where a brand idea becomes a physical product. The process works best when the buyer and factory agree on what is being decided at each stage: the brief, the technical specification, the sample, the revision and the production standard. Skipping a stage usually saves a day in the short term and creates a larger delay later.

This guide follows a practical sequence for an OEM or ODM handbag programme. It is written for buyers who have a sketch, a reference image or a completed design and need to turn it into a sample they can approve with confidence.

1. Write a sourcing brief before asking for a price

The first brief does not need to be a full tech pack, but it should explain the commercial and physical problem the bag has to solve. A factory can propose a construction only when it understands the use case, the target customer and the price position.

Brief fieldWhat to send
Product typeTote, crossbody, shoulder bag, top-handle, sling or another format
Use caseRetail, gifting, travel, corporate, hospitality or promotional programme
Target customerMass market, boutique, premium or a specific brand positioning
SizeWidth, height, depth and strap drop in centimetres
MaterialsFull PU, PU-trimmed, canvas, linen or a combination, with reference images
BrandingLogo method, placement, colour reference and whether the logo is embossed, printed or metal
CommercialsTarget quantity, target unit price, sample deadline and launch date

2. Turn the brief into a tech pack

A tech pack is the document the sampling room and the production line work from. It does not need to be beautiful, but it must be unambiguous. Include a front, back, side, top and bottom view, with notes for every feature that changes the construction.

At minimum, specify the finished dimensions, strap drop, gusset depth, pocket locations, closure type, lining, interlining, edge finish, stitch detail, hardware finish and logo placement. Add a measurement tolerance and a colour reference for each material. If a detail is cosmetic rather than functional, say so; the factory can then suggest a lower-cost way to achieve the same look.

Reference images are useful, but they are not a specification. Mark up the image and explain what you want to keep and what you want to change. “Make it like this” is not enough when the reference has a different proportion, hardware or internal layout.

3. Agree the commercial basis

Before sampling, confirm the MOQ, sample fee, tooling or plate cost, payment terms and the point at which production lead time starts. For standard handbag styles, MOQ starts from 100 pcs. Custom development starts from 300 pcs. Standard-style samples are free; custom samples are USD 60–150 depending on the construction.

The custom sample fee is refunded when the order is placed or when cumulative quantity reaches 1,000 pcs. Put that in the quotation rather than relying on a verbal understanding. Also confirm who pays courier transit and whether the sample will be sent by DHL, FedEx or your own account.

4. Build and review the first sample

Sampling takes 3–5 days after artwork approval. The first sample is not a formality; it is the best opportunity to find problems while changes are still inexpensive. Review it in the same order every time so nothing gets missed.

  • Proportion: does the bag match the drawing when it is carried, filled and placed on a table?
  • Structure: does it stand, slouch or fold in the way the design intended?
  • Materials: does the PU or textile feel right, and is the colour acceptable under daylight?
  • Stitching: check stitch density, seam alignment, back-stitching and loose threads.
  • Hardware: open and close every zip, clasp, magnet and ring; check the finish and attachment.
  • Strap: measure the drop, test the anchors and confirm the adjustment range.
  • Lining and pockets: check access, pocket depth, seam coverage and any card or phone slots.
  • Branding: compare placement, size, colour and edge quality with the approved artwork.
  • Weight and packing: weigh the sample and confirm how it will be protected in the carton.

5. Consolidate comments and approve a golden sample

Send one consolidated comment sheet rather than a stream of separate messages. Number each comment and attach a photo or a marked-up drawing. Separate must-fix issues from optional improvements so the sampling team can prioritise correctly.

Once the sample is correct, approve it in writing and keep a signed or photographed golden sample. The golden sample becomes the reference for bulk production, final inspection and any later claim. If the buyer changes a material or hardware item after approval, treat it as a new revision and confirm the effect on cost and lead time.

6. Move from sample approval to bulk production

Bulk production takes 15–25 days after sample approval. The clock should start only when the approved sample, deposit, artwork and any customer-supplied materials are in place. Before the line starts, hold a pre-production review covering the bill of materials, measurements, logo placement, packing method and inspection criteria.

During production, check the first output against the golden sample, then run in-line checks and a final inspection before cartons are sealed. A practical QC checklist should cover materials, stitching, hardware, branding, measurements, packing and AQL sampling. Our bag factory QC checklist explains the checks in more detail.

Why handbag development slips

Most delays come from decisions made late, not from sewing speed. Common causes include unclear dimensions, artwork that arrives after sampling begins, hardware that is out of stock, a material change after the first sample, or approval by a committee without one final decision-maker. A short written approval at each gate prevents most of these problems.

If the launch date is fixed, work backwards from the delivery date and protect time for sample review, courier transit and any revision. A sample that arrives late but is correct is usually cheaper than a production run that starts before the specification is settled.

Send the right starting file

You do not need a finished tech pack to begin. Send the sketch or reference image, the dimensions you know, the material direction, the logo artwork, the target quantity and the date you need the goods. We can help turn that into a sampling brief and quote.
