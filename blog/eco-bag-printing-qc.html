Eco Bag Printing & QC: Getting Your Logo Right at Every Volume

From artwork to container — how jute, canvas and PP bags are decorated, and the checks that keep prints and handles from failing.

Your logo is the whole point of the bag. Yet printing and quality control are where eco-bag programs most often go wrong: prints peel after a few washes, colors shift between batches, or a handle rips out under a full load. This guide covers how eco bags are actually decorated and what we inspect before anything ships.

Pick the Print by Volume and Look

  • Screen print — the eco-bag classic. Sharp, opaque color at low unit cost, from one color on jute up to six colors on both sides of non-woven PP.
  • Flexo printing — the volume champion on woven PP and jute reels, up to six colors with clean registration on simple logos.
  • Heat transfer — full-color and gradient artwork without screen setup; ideal for photo graphics on canvas and non-woven panels.
  • DTG and embroidery — DTG gives photo-quality results on cotton canvas; embroidery adds a premium stitched logo at higher cost.
  • Jacquard — the logo is woven into the fabric itself; durable and elegant, economical only at very large volumes.
Ink matters as much as the method. We default to water-based inks on eco programs — they cure into the fabric, keep the print breathable and avoid the stiff plastisol look buyers increasingly reject.

Where Your Logo Can Live

  • Front and back panels take the largest, flattest print area
  • Side gussets suit smaller logos and short slogans
  • Handle areas need placement guides — ink on a stress point is an invitation to peel
  • Fabric labels, woven patches and hang tags carry care and compliance copy

Review the artwork guides on our canvas tote, non-woven and woven PP pages when you plan your decoration.

QC Checks Specific to Eco Bags

  • Handle-pull and load test — non-woven shoppers are load-tested to 8–10 kg; jute and canvas get strap-pull and seam-strength checks at the handle junction
  • Print adhesion — tape-pull and rub tests catch under-cured ink before it fails in the field
  • Stitch density and bar-tacks — stress points on handles and base must match the approved sample
  • Weight and gsm tolerance — fabric weight drives cost and feel, so we verify it on arrival and mid-production
  • Dye-lot consistency — jute and canvas are natural fibres that vary by harvest; we cut from matched lots
  • Size tolerance — panel measurements checked across the run, not just the first carton
  • Moisture and odour check — jute and BOPP-laminated woven PP can trap moisture in transit; cartons are checked before loading

Reports You Can Request

Every program ships with a pre-shipment inspection you can join live or receive as a report. Ask us for an AQL 2.5 inspection, load-test results, and third-party testing through SGS or Intertek whenever your buyer or retailer requires documentation.

Before You Confirm Production

  1. Approve a Pantone-matched print proof, not just a PDF preview
  2. Sign off the physical sample, including handle reinforcement
  3. Agree on print placement and the maximum print area
  4. Confirm ink and material requirements if bags touch food-adjacent goods
  5. Set weight/gsm tolerances and your inspection level

Walk through these points with our QC team and the approved sample becomes the enforceable standard for the whole run.
