PU, Canvas or Linen: Which Handbag Material Fits Your Brand?

Nov 25, 2026 • Handbag Sourcing Guide

Material choice decides how a handbag looks, how it holds its shape, how it wears and how much it costs to produce. It also changes the words you should use in your product listing. A bag described as “PU leather” is not the same product as a canvas bag with PU-trimmed handles, even when the two samples sit side by side on a buyer’s desk.

For a custom programme, start with the construction rather than a single material name. The most useful brief tells the factory which parts are visible, which parts carry load, how the edges are finished and what the target retail price is. That gives the sampling team enough information to propose a construction instead of guessing from a reference photo.

Three material directions buyers usually compare

1. Full PU leather outer shell

Use “PU leather” when the outer shell of the bag is made from polyurethane material. Full PU shells are common for structured totes, crossbody bags and fashion handbags because they can be wiped clean, colour-matched and embossed. The result depends on the base fabric, the PU coating, the backing, the thickness and the finishing at the edges.

Two bags can both be called PU leather and still feel completely different. One may be thin and soft; another may be thick, rigid and embossed. Ask for the material weight or thickness, the backing, the surface finish and the colour reference. If the bag has painted edges, folded edges or raw edges, confirm that in the sample comment sheet. These details affect both the look and the production cost.

2. PU-trimmed canvas or nylon

“PU-trimmed” is the clearer description when the body is canvas, nylon or another textile and PU appears only on handles, panels, piping or reinforcement. This construction can give a bag more structure without covering the whole shell in a synthetic finish. It also lets a brand combine a printed textile body with wipe-clean handles or a crisp contrast panel.

Be precise in the specification. If only the handles are PU, write “canvas body with PU-trimmed handles.” If the front panel is PU but the gusset is canvas, say so. Clear part-by-part language prevents a sampling misunderstanding and keeps the product description honest.

3. Canvas and linen bodies

Cotton canvas is a strong choice for soft totes, casual handbags and lifestyle collections. Heavier canvas gives a more structured shape; a washed finish gives a softer, more relaxed hand feel. Coated canvas can add surface protection and make the bag easier to clean, but the coating, backing and folding method all need to be confirmed on the sample.

Linen and cotton-linen blends bring a natural texture that works well for summer collections, resort programmes and gift ranges. They are usually softer and more prone to creasing than a structured PU shell. If the design needs a crisp silhouette, discuss interlining and reinforcement rather than assuming the outer fabric alone will hold the shape.

What the material decision changes in production

Material selection is not only a visual decision. It changes the cutting plan, the seam allowance, the interlining, the needle and thread choice, the edge finish and the final inspection criteria. A factory can only quote accurately when those details are part of the brief.

  • Structure: a full PU shell may need a different reinforcement package from a soft canvas body.
  • Hardware: strap anchors, rings, magnetic closures and zips should be chosen together with the shell material.
  • Branding: embossing, foil stamping, metal plates, embroidery and screen printing each suit different surfaces.
  • Lining: the lining affects the hand feel, internal pockets, closure installation and weight.
  • Packing: structured bags may need more protection, inserts or individual dust bags than soft totes.

Put these questions in your RFQ

A useful request for quotation should be short enough to read and specific enough to cost. Send reference images, but also answer these questions:

  1. Is the outer shell full PU, PU-trimmed or textile?
  2. What are the width, height, depth and strap drop in centimetres?
  3. Which parts are visible, and which parts carry the most load?
  4. What lining, interlining and internal pockets do you need?
  5. Which hardware finish and closure type are acceptable?
  6. How should the logo be applied, and where?
  7. What is the target quantity, target price and launch date?

If you already have a technical drawing, send it. If you only have a mood board, say which details are fixed and which are open. That distinction helps the factory propose a workable construction instead of copying a detail that cannot be produced at your target cost.

MOQ, sampling and the first production run

For Verlora handbag programmes, standard styles start from 100 pcs and custom development starts from 300 pcs. Standard-style samples are free; custom samples are USD 60–150 depending on the construction. The sample fee is refunded when the order is placed or when cumulative quantity reaches 1,000 pcs.

Sampling takes 3–5 days after artwork approval. Bulk production takes 15–25 days after sample approval. Courier transit is separate and can be arranged by DHL, FedEx or your nominated carrier. Those dates should be written into the quotation so everyone is planning from the same trigger point.

Choose the material that supports the brief

The best material is not automatically the most expensive one. It is the one that delivers the required shape, hand feel, branding and durability at a cost the programme can support. A full PU shell may be right for a structured fashion bag; a canvas body with PU-trimmed handles may be the better answer for a lighter lifestyle tote; linen may be the right choice when texture and seasonality matter more than rigidity.
